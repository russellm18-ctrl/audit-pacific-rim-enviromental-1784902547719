## Atmospheric Reality Gap

Your four hero images are doing active damage. The rotating banner opens with extreme close-up of peeling, rotting blue paint — a texture that reads decay before a buyer has processed a single word of copy. The second frame is a flat, overcast shot of a building wrapped in plastic sheeting, photographed from across a wet parking lot with no subject, no human presence, no implied expertise. Frame three shows a worker in a hazmat suit standing under a barnacle-crusted ship hull in harsh midday blowout — the composition is documentary at best, unvetted jobsite photo at worst. Frame four is a shallow-focus desk shot with a miniature Space Needle souvenir in the foreground, which visually undercuts the lab credibility the copy works hard to build.

Your headline reads "Redefining Consulting for 30 Years." Your images transmit: we grabbed whatever was on someone's phone. That gap is what a facilities director or project manager feels in the first four seconds — and it hands the shortlist position to whoever looks like they've done this before.

The trust cost: environmental consulting is a liability-transfer purchase. Buyers are asking whether they can stake their compliance record on your judgment. Visual chaos answers that question before you speak.

## Visual Narrative Coherence

The scan confirmed 20 of 24 homepage links are broken — including every single service page linked from your navigation: `/service/`, `/service/hazardous-materials-management/`, `/service/indoor-air-quality/`, `/service/industrial-hygiene/`, `/service/laboratory-services/`, and `/service/leed-building-certification-credits/` are all unreachable. A buyer clicks Hazardous Materials Management from the dropdown and hits a dead wall. Then clicks Indoor Air Quality — same result. That pattern doesn't read as a technical glitch; it reads as a company that isn't operational.

The homepage itself (`https://pacrimenv.com/`) is flagged unreachable in the scan, meaning crawlers and buyers arriving from any referral link may be hitting an error state before the hero images even load. You rank #1 for your own brand name, which means traffic is arriving — and the site is failing to receive it.

For a high-value buyer comparing three vendors, a broken service menu is disqualifying. They don't file a bug report. They leave.

## Metadata and Conversion Gaps

74% of your 19 content images — 14 out of 19 — carry no alt text. That means search engines cannot read what your photography depicts, which kills any chance of ranking for image search terms like "asbestos abatement inspection" or "industrial hygiene assessment." The filename `rrv-IMG_0302-cropped-1024x583-1-2-1210x423.jpg` is a camera-roll export passed directly into production — it signals nothing to Google and actively dilutes what little image SEO equity the page might otherwise build.

Your meta description is missing entirely. Google is auto-generating a snippet from whatever body text it pulls first — you have no control over what promise is made to a searcher before they click. Your page title at 37 characters is functional but generic: "Home - Pacific Rim Environmental Inc." states identity, not value. Combined with a missing meta description, you are forfeiting the one line of copy that converts a ranked position into an actual visit.

You rank #1 for your brand name, which means the audience is finding you. The site is then failing to hold them, route them, or close them — at the navigation level, the metadata level, and the visual credibility level simultaneously.
